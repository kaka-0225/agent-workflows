# Book Reading Rules

Use these rules when applying the `book-read` workflow.

## Language

- Default user-facing responses should be in Chinese.
- Keep book titles, author names, chapter titles, quoted text, and important
  terms in their original language when useful.
- Translate English passages into faithful, natural Chinese when using
  `passage-read`.

## Reading Modes

- `passage-read`: original text preservation and translation only.
- `chapter-understand`: chapter or section explanation.
- `theme-analysis`: analysis of a recurring theme.
- `quote-reflection`: close reading and reflection on a quote.
- `personal-reflection`: reader-centered reflection without diagnosis.
- `reading-summary`: staged or final notes.

Do not mix these modes unless the user explicitly asks.

## Literal Reading Rules

For `passage-read`:

- Do not summarize.
- Do not analyze.
- Do not shorten.
- Do not add advice.
- Preserve paragraph structure when possible.
- Mark OCR, PDF extraction, missing text, or page boundary uncertainty.

## Interpretation Rules

- Separate original text, translation, author viewpoint, agent explanation, and
  user reflection.
- Do not invent content from memory, title, reputation, or online summaries.
- Do not treat the author's viewpoint as absolute truth.
- Do not turn a reflective passage into a diagnosis.

## Sensitive Topic Rules

For themes such as intimacy, shame, self-worth, desire, anger, relationships,
family, sexuality, and trauma:

- Use respectful and non-judgmental language.
- Avoid clinical certainty.
- Offer questions and possibilities rather than verdicts.
- Encourage human support when the topic appears high-stakes or distressing.

## Copyright Boundary

- Work with text the user provides or files available in the workspace.
- Do not reconstruct full copyrighted books from memory.
- Keep quoted excerpts brief in summaries unless transforming user-provided
  text.
